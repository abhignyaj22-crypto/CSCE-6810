# Algorithm Scout (Vision and ML Venues): Per-Cell Gene-Program Prediction from DAPI and H&E in Bone Marrow

Scout date: 2026-11-03
Scope: peer-reviewed algorithms from ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS and CVPR main conferences (2023 to 2026). TMLR is admitted only where explicitly flagged. Workshop papers and "Findings" tracks are listed only as excluded or borderline items and are never recommended.

Verification method: for every VERIFIED item the venue page (openaccess.thecvf.com, proceedings.neurips.cc, proceedings.mlr.press, proceedings.iclr.cc, or the official iclr.cc / neurips.cc / cvpr.thecvf.com virtual page) was opened with WebFetch and the title, author list, venue and year were copied from that page. OpenReview forum pages could not be opened from this environment (a browser verification wall and a blocked API host), so no venue claim below rests on OpenReview. Items whose venue could not be confirmed from an opened page are marked UNVERIFIED and are described only from the abstract page that was read. Method details beyond the abstract are given only where the full PDF or HTML was opened, and that is stated per item.

Target model recap (for fit assessment): input is nuclear morphology (DAPI) and H&E of bone marrow trephines; output is per-cell gene-program scores and spatial gene programs; supervision is Xenium 5K spatial transcriptomics; evaluation must be patient-held-out.

### Per-item notes

A1. TANGLE (CVPR 2024). Method (from the PDF): a frozen patch encoder (CTransPath for human tissue, an iBOT ViT-B for rat) produces patch embeddings; an attention-based MIL (ABMIL) pools them to a slide embedding; a 3-layer MLP maps a bulk expression vector (log2 fold change, min-max normalised, top 1,000 genes for liver) to an expression embedding of the same dimension; the two are aligned with a symmetric contrastive loss over slide-expression pairs in a batch. Variants add an L2 reconstruction head (TANGLE-Rec, +0.05% AUC in ablation) and a vision-only intra-modality term (which hurt by 0.8%). Replacing the contrastive loss with L1 or L2 cost 6.7% and 7.0% AUC. Input and output: slide (bag of patches) plus bulk expression in; a slide embedding out. What is reusable at cell level: the recipe itself transfers directly if "slide" is replaced by "cell neighbourhood crop" and "bulk expression" by "Xenium per-cell program score vector". The symmetric contrastive objective with in-batch negatives is the piece to adopt, with the ablation evidence that contrastive beats direct regression for the alignment stage. Faithful adaptation: a cell-centred H&E or DAPI crop encoded by a frozen encoder, a small MLP on the program-score vector, InfoNCE across cells in a batch sampled from many patients, then a linear or MLP regression head on the aligned image embedding. Risk: TANGLE was trained on thousands of slide-level pairs; at the cell level the positives are far noisier (segmentation error, transcript misassignment), so a temperature sweep and hard-negative control by patient will matter. Compute: light, the encoder is frozen.

A2. PANTHER (CVPR 2024). Method (abstract): fit a Gaussian mixture model over a slide's frozen patch embeddings; the mixture parameters are the slide representation, each component acting as a morphological prototype. Unsupervised, task agnostic; matched or beat supervised MIL on 13 datasets. Reusable for us: for the spatial-program output (programs that live at the neighbourhood scale), a GMM over the cell embeddings inside a neighbourhood gives a compact, interpretable set of "cell-state prototypes" per neighbourhood. Faithful adaptation: prototypes fitted on training patients only; per-neighbourhood posterior mixing weights as features for the spatial-program head. Risk: prototype count is a hyperparameter and bone marrow is cell dense, so a GMM on nuclear-level embeddings may need many components. Compute: CPU-level after embedding extraction.

A3. MMP (ICML 2024). Method (abstract): compress the whole-slide patch set into a small set of morphological prototypes (more than 300x fewer tokens) and the transcriptome into biological pathway prototypes, both unsupervised, then fuse with a Transformer or an optimal-transport cross-alignment. Reusable: the pathway-prototype idea maps exactly onto "gene programs" as tokens, so a program-token decoder with cross-attention from cell or neighbourhood tokens to program tokens is a faithful adaptation and gives per-program attention maps for interpretation. Risk: MMP is slide level and survival supervised; we use it only as the token design, not the objective. Compute: small after prototyping.

A4. SurvPath (CVPR 2024). Method (abstract): pathway tokens from gene expression plus histology patch tokens fused by a memory-efficient multimodal Transformer, with an interpretability framework linking pathways to morphology. Reusable: the pathway-token construction and the attention-based interpretability. Risk: requires expression at inference in its native form; for us it is a training-time teacher only (expression tokens available only for Xenium-paired training cells). Compute: not read.

A5. COBRA (CVPR 2025). Method (abstract): contrastive self-supervised slide encoder in feature space using tiles from several foundation models and a Mamba-2 architecture; works on unseen feature extractors. Reusable: the idea of ensembling several frozen patch encoders' features before a light contrastive head. Risk: slide level; our cell-level use is an extrapolation. Compute: pretrained on 3,048 slides, so modest.

A6. HEST-1k (NeurIPS 2024 D&B). Listed here because the HEST-Benchmark is the only venue-published, patient-stratified comparison of frozen pathology encoders on expression prediction. Protocol (arXiv HTML read): 112 um H&E regions (224 px at 20x), top 50 highest normalised-variance genes, log1p expression, PCA to 256 then ridge, patient-level k-fold, Pearson averaged across folds. Mean Pearson across nine tasks: ResNet50-ImageNet 0.326; CTransPath 0.345; Phikon 0.366; CONCH 0.371; REMEDIS 0.374; GigaPath 0.385; UNI 0.386; Virchow 0.398; Virchow2 0.398; UNIv1.5 0.409; H-Optimus-0 0.415. Note that the encoders themselves (UNI, CONCH, Virchow, H-Optimus, GigaPath) are published in journals or as preprints, not at the venues in scope; only the benchmark is. Reusable: the exact protocol as our minimum baseline (frozen encoder, PCA, ridge, patient folds) and the metric ranges as a sanity anchor. Risk: Visium spot scale, not Xenium cell scale.

A7. DINOv2 (TMLR, flagged; venue UNVERIFIED here). Abstract: self-supervised ViT trained on a curated large image set, 1B-parameter model distilled to smaller ones, outperforming OpenCLIP on most benchmarks. Reusable: generic frozen backbone and the distillation recipe; no histology specificity. Risk: natural-image pretraining; HEST-Benchmark shows ImageNet-style features trail pathology encoders by roughly 0.05 to 0.09 mean Pearson.

A8. LEMON (arXiv 2026, UNVERIFIED, not peer reviewed). Abstract: self-supervised foundation model for single-cell image representation trained on millions of cell images from diverse tissues; weights on Hugging Face. It is the closest thing found to a nuclear-morphology encoder and is therefore relevant to the DAPI branch, but it fails the venue rule and no metrics were read. Listed only as a fallback candidate to test, not to cite as a peer-reviewed method.

### Recommendation A
Adopt: (1) a frozen pathology patch encoder selected by rerunning the HEST-Benchmark protocol on our own Xenium-paired cells (A6); (2) a TANGLE-style symmetric contrastive alignment between cell-centred image crops and Xenium program-score vectors, followed by a regression head (A1); (3) PANTHER or MMP prototype tokens for the neighbourhood-scale spatial-program head (A2, A3). Fallback outside the venue list: DINOv2 as a generic backbone (TMLR, flagged) and LEMON for DAPI nuclei (preprint). Plain statement: no CVPR, ICML, ICLR or NeurIPS main-conference paper was found that publishes a nucleus-level (single-cell crop) self-supervised encoder for histology; the venue papers above are all patch or slide level and the cell-level use is our adaptation.

### Per-item notes

B1. Point Transformer V3 (CVPR 2024). Method (abstract): drop exact kNN neighbour search in favour of serialized neighbour mapping (space-filling-curve ordering), which lets attention run over windows of 1024 points instead of 16, with large speed and memory gains. Input and output: point cloud with per-point features in, per-point features out. Fit: a bone marrow section is a 2D point cloud of cell centroids with image-derived features; PTv3 is the most direct venue-published way to run attention over hundreds of thousands of cells per section. Adopt: serialization over cell centroids (Hilbert or Z order on the 2D coordinates), windowed attention, and the PTv3 encoder-decoder for per-cell outputs. Risk: designed for 3D scans, so positional encodings and patch-grid parameters must be re-tuned for 2D micron coordinates; serialization windows cut across tissue structures at window borders (PTv3 mitigates with shifted orders). Compute: reported 3x speed and 10x memory improvement over PTv2.

B2. Exphormer (ICML 2023). Method (abstract): sparse attention over the union of input-graph edges, expander-graph edges and virtual global nodes; linear complexity; competitive within GraphGPS. Fit: if we build an explicit kNN or Delaunay cell graph, Exphormer gives global mixing without dense attention and keeps the graph edges as local inductive bias. Adopt: Exphormer attention as the graph-transformer block over the kNN cell graph, with the virtual global node serving as a per-section context token. Risk: the expander edges are random and not biologically meaningful; interpretability of attention weights is weaker than a pure kNN attention. Compute: linear in nodes plus edges.

B3. FlashAttention-2 (ICLR 2024). Method (abstract): exact attention with better GPU work partitioning; about 2x faster than FlashAttention and reaching 50 to 73% of theoretical peak on A100. Fit: the kernel is the implementation substrate for whichever attention design is chosen (PTv3 windows, Exphormer blocks, or plain kNN attention); it changes nothing in the model. Adopt as a dependency. Risk: none at the method level; requires compatible GPU and dtype.

B4. HEIST (ICLR 2026). Method (abstract): hierarchical graph transformer; tissue as a graph of spatially linked cells, each cell carrying a gene co-expression network; gene embeddings computed from the co-expression network and cellular context rather than a fixed vocabulary; pretrained on 22.3M cells, 124 tissues, 15 organs; evaluated on clinical outcome prediction, cell type annotation and gene imputation. Fit: HEIST is the only verified main-conference model built for spatial omics cell graphs, and its hierarchical (cell graph plus gene graph) design is the right reference for a spatial-program head. For our model, HEIST cannot be used at inference because it consumes expression, but it can be a training-time teacher: HEIST embeddings of Xenium cells can supervise the image-side embeddings (distillation), and its niche-level outputs can define the spatial-program targets. Risk: bone marrow coverage in its pretraining set is unknown from the abstract; distillation adds a dependency on a large external model. Compute: pretraining is heavy and not ours; inference over our cells is moderate.

B5. CellPLM (ICLR 2024). Method (abstract): cells as tokens, tissues as sentences; pretrained with spatially resolved transcriptomics to learn cell-cell relations; Gaussian prior as inductive bias; about 100x faster embedding than prior pretrained models. Fit: same teacher role as HEIST, with a lighter model. Risk: the spatial data it was trained on and its gene vocabulary may not match Xenium 5K; abstract does not say. Compute: light.

B6. SGFormer (NeurIPS 2023). Method (abstract): one global attention layer, no positional encodings or preprocessing, scales to 100M-node graphs. Fit: a cheap single-layer global mixing block for whole-section context, to be stacked after local kNN attention. Risk: a single global layer may be too coarse for niche-scale structure; use as an ablation arm. Compute: very low.

### Recommendation B
Adopt: a two-scale design. Local scale: PTv3-style serialized windowed attention over cell centroids (B1), or equivalently kNN attention on an explicit cell graph with Exphormer sparse mixing (B2), both run on FlashAttention-2 kernels (B3). Teacher scale: HEIST (B4) or CellPLM (B5) embeddings on the Xenium training cells to define and regularise the spatial-program targets. No fallback outside the venue list is needed.

### Per-item notes

C1. TRIPLEX (CVPR 2024). Method (PDF read): three feature streams, the target spot patch, its local neighbourhood and global slide context, fused to predict 250 genes per dataset. Datasets: BC1 (36 samples, 8 patients), BC2 (68 samples, 23 patients), SCC (12 samples, 4 patients), plus three external 10x Visium breast sets. Metrics: PCC(M) is the mean per-gene Pearson across all 250 genes; PCC(H) is the mean over the top 50 most predictable genes. TRIPLEX: BC1 0.314 / 0.497, BC2 0.206 / 0.352, SCC 0.374 / 0.490, against the best baseline EGN at 0.197 / 0.328 on BC1. Input and output: H&E patch set in, per-spot expression vector out. Adopt: the three-scale decomposition maps onto our cell crop (target), kNN neighbourhood (local) and section-level token (global). Risk: spot scale (55 um Visium) and few patients; cross-validation folds were not all patient-disjoint in the sense we require, so treat the numbers as upper-range anchors. Compute: a frozen patch encoder plus small fusion transformers; modest.

C2. MERGE (CVPR 2025). Method (PDF read): ResNet18 pretrained on TCGA-BRCA, two linear layers to 256-d, fine-tuned with MSE; spots clustered both spatially and in feature space; each cluster centroid connected to its members, centroids fully connected (shortcut edges), plus 8-neighbour grid edges; 4 GATConv layers, 8 heads, layer norm, edge dropout 0.2, MSE loss. Also proposes gene-aware smoothing of noisy ST targets. PCC: ST-Net 0.6795, Her2ST 0.5037, SCC 0.5512 (the hierarchical model); 1-hop-only ablation 0.5789 / 0.4866 / 0.4843. Note that in MERGE's own table TRIPLEX scores 0.232 / 0.393 / 0.542, so protocols differ between papers and numbers are not comparable across C1 and C2. Adopt: the dual (spatial plus morphological) clustering to add long-range edges to the cell graph, and the point that target smoothing changes the ceiling. Risk: target smoothing inflates PCC relative to raw counts; we should report both raw and smoothed targets. Compute: light (ResNet18 plus a 4-layer GAT).

C3. MCToGene (CVPR 2026). Abstract: many-body attention that models multi-cell (many-to-many) interactions, a hierarchical module coupling pairwise and many-body representations to keep memory manageable; 7.85% relative improvement on HEST-1k and STImage-1K4M. Adopt: as the venue precedent for higher-order cell interaction terms; only after a pairwise kNN attention baseline is in place. Risk: method details and absolute metrics were not read (PDF fetch failed). Compute: not read.

C4. HEST-Benchmark (NeurIPS 2024 D&B). See A6 for the protocol and numbers. This is the expectation anchor: with a frozen encoder and ridge, mean Pearson on the 50 most variable genes is 0.33 to 0.41 at the Visium spot scale under patient-held-out folds. Per-cell Xenium targets are noisier than spot pseudobulk, so program-level (not gene-level) targets and neighbourhood aggregation are the two levers expected to keep us in this range rather than below it.

C5. BLEEP (NeurIPS 2023). Abstract: contrastive alignment of patch and expression into a shared space, then expression of a query patch is estimated from the nearest reference pairs (retrieval). Adopt: as the retrieval baseline and as a sanity check for the contrastive stage of A1. Risk: retrieval cannot extrapolate beyond the reference set; in TRIPLEX's tables BLEEP is below ST-Net on PCC(M).

C6. PixNet (CVPR 2026 Highlight). Abstract: predict a dense, continuous expression map from the image and aggregate inside spots of any size. Adopt: the dense-map formulation is the natural route to a per-cell readout (aggregate inside each nucleus mask) and to spot-size-agnostic training. Risk: no numbers read; main-proceedings page was not opened, only the virtual page.

C7. Stem (UNVERIFIED venue). Abstract page: conditional diffusion (DiT, 12 blocks, 384-d) on top of frozen pathology features, averaged over about 20 samples at inference; proposes Relative Variation Distance to check that predicted per-gene variance matches truth. Listed only because the variance-preservation metric is a good addition to our evaluation; do not cite as a venue method.

### Recommendation C
Adopt: TRIPLEX's three-scale decomposition (C1) and MERGE's long-range hierarchical edges (C2) as the architecture pattern over the cell graph chosen in B; the HEST-Benchmark protocol and its 0.33 to 0.41 range (C4) as the baseline and expectation; BLEEP retrieval (C5) as the floor baseline. Plain statement: none of the verified venue methods predicts at single-cell resolution on Xenium; all are Visium spot level. The cell-level step is our contribution and should be reported with both per-cell and neighbourhood-aggregated Pearson, per program, under patient-held-out folds.

### Per-item notes

D1. confusion-GAN (CVPR 2024). Abstract: unpaired H&E to IHC translation with a multi-branch discriminator and a MIL-guided patch-level pathology extractor to preserve diagnostic content; evaluated on three IHC types, with pathologists fooled by the outputs. Adopt only the structural idea: an unpaired translator from DAPI to H&E-like images with a content-preservation constraint supplied by a frozen pathology encoder, so a single H&E-trained program predictor can be applied to DAPI inputs. Risk: translation hallucinates cytoplasmic and stromal content that DAPI does not contain, and the paper's validation is diagnostic classification, not expression regression. For our purpose the translator would have to be validated by program-score Pearson, not by realism. Compute: GAN training, moderate.

D2. SAR (ICLR 2023). Abstract: test-time adaptation by entropy minimisation made stable by discarding high-gradient samples and seeking flat minima; shows batch norm as the main instability source and favours batch-agnostic norms. Adopt: the practical lesson that a model intended for test-time or cross-site adaptation should use layer or group norm rather than batch norm, and that any TTA should filter unreliable samples. Risk: the entropy objective applies to classifiers; there is no direct regression analogue in this paper. Compute: negligible.

D3. TT-SaD (ECCV 2024, out of scope, fallback). Abstract: treat stain shift as a nonlinear inverse problem solved with a diffusion prior at test time; trains on one domain and adapts to unseen stains without retraining. Fallback for H&E colour and scanner shift across sites. It does not address DAPI.

### Recommendation D
Plain statement: there is no fitting ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS or CVPR main-conference method for DAPI-to-H&E transfer. Recommended practice, not drawn from a single venue paper: train the per-cell model on paired DAPI and H&E crops of the same nuclei (available because Xenium sections carry DAPI and the H&E is registered), with modality dropout so that either input alone yields a prediction, and with standard stain and intensity augmentation; use layer or group norm (lesson from D2). If a DAPI-only deployment must match an H&E-trained predictor, confusion-GAN's unpaired translation with a frozen-encoder content constraint (D1) is the only venue precedent and must be validated on program-score Pearson. TT-SaD (D3, ECCV 2024) is the fallback for H&E stain shift across sites.

### Per-item notes

E1. Conformal Risk Control (ICLR 2024). Abstract: extends split conformal to control the expectation of any monotone loss, tight to O(1/n), with extensions to distribution shift, quantile risk and multiple risks. Adopt: calibrate a per-cell interval half-width (or a per-program miscoverage loss) on calibration cells drawn from held-out calibration patients. For patient-level exchangeability the unit of calibration must be the patient: compute one score per patient (for example the patient's mean or a high quantile of per-cell residuals) so that the exchangeable units are patients, not cells. Risk: with roughly 20 patients the 1/n slack is large, so guarantees will be coarse (coverage targets near 0.8 rather than 0.95). Compute: negligible.

E2. Kandinsky CP (ICML 2025). Abstract: conditional coverage for overlapping and fractional groups defined jointly on covariates and labels, unifying group-conditional, class-conditional and Mondrian conformal prediction, with a minimax-optimal high-probability bound. Adopt: groups equal to patient, cell type and tissue region (which overlap), so that coverage is reported and enforced per patient and per cell type rather than only marginally. Risk: more groups means more calibration data per group; with few patients, patient-wise conditional coverage will be loose. Compute: a linear program or similar over calibration scores; light.

E3. Regression-as-Classification (ICLR 2024). Abstract: bin the continuous target, train a classifier with an order-preserving loss, apply classification conformal to get regression sets; robust to heteroscedastic, multimodal and skewed outputs. Adopt: program scores are often zero-inflated or bimodal (program on or off), so this is the natural conformal wrapper for the prediction head. Risk: produces sets that may be unions of intervals; interpretability needs care.

E4. PLCP (ICML 2024). Abstract: learn uncertainty-aware partitions from calibration data by alternating gradient descent, giving finite and infinite sample conditional guarantees. Adopt as an optional refinement to E2 when groups are not known in advance. Risk: learned partitions on small calibration sets can overfit.

E5. Multi-output conformal regression (ICML 2025). Abstract: nine methods compared in one framework on 13 tabular datasets, plus two new conformity-score classes with asymptotic conditional and exact finite-sample marginal coverage, one for any generative model and one for invertible generative models. Adopt: if a joint region over the whole program vector is wanted, use its generalized scores; otherwise per-program intervals with a Bonferroni or risk-control correction (E1). Risk: joint regions in high dimensions are wide.

E6. Lee, Barber and Willett (UNVERIFIED venue). Abstract: a hierarchical form of exchangeability for nested or grouped data (clusters, repeated measurements) with conformal and jackknife+ extensions, and a stronger "second-moment coverage" that controls conditional miscoverage; sets widen when the model is poorly calibrated. This is the exact statistical setting of cells nested in patients and is recommended as the methodological reference, with the explicit caveat that no ICML, ICLR, NeurIPS or CVPR page confirming publication was opened.

E7. Principato et al. (UNVERIFIED, poor fit). Listed to prevent a naming confusion: its hierarchy is a set of linear aggregation constraints (regional totals summing to national), not patients containing cells. Not recommended.

### Recommendation E
Adopt: patient-level split conformal where the exchangeable unit is the patient, implemented with Conformal Risk Control (E1) for per-program interval calibration, Kandinsky group-conditional calibration (E2) to report coverage per patient and per cell type, and Regression-as-Classification (E3) as the conformal wrapper for zero-inflated program scores. Fallback outside the venue list: the hierarchical exchangeability construction of Lee, Barber and Willett (E6) for the formal patient-nesting guarantee. Plain statement: no verified ICML, ICLR or NeurIPS 2023 to 2025 paper found in this scout gives a conformal method whose stated guarantee is specifically for clustered (patient-nested) regression; the venue methods above assume exchangeable units and must be applied at the patient level to be valid.
