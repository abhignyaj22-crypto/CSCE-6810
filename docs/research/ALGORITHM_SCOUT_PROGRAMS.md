# Algorithm Scout: Inferring and Interpreting Gene Programs in Myeloma Bone Marrow

Prepared 2026-11-03. Scope: peer-reviewed methods from the main conferences of ICML, ICLR and NeurIPS (2023 to 2026) for (A) gene program inference with prior knowledge, (B) spatially informed program inference, (C) pathway-level interpretation of a learned program, (D) false-discovery-controlled gene selection, and (E) multi-output count regression; plus biology-venue and statistics fallbacks.

Verification rule applied to every entry. For each top-venue candidate the proceedings page (proceedings.neurips.cc, proceedings.iclr.cc, proceedings.mlr.press) or the OpenReview record (read through the OpenReview API, which reports the accepted venue field such as "ICLR 2025 Poster" or "NeurIPS 2025 poster") was opened and the title, authors and venue field were copied from it. Where a page could not be opened, the entry is marked UNVERIFIED. Biology-venue fallbacks were verified through PubMed and are attributed "According to PubMed" with DOI links. Nothing below was taken from memory alone.

Target setting for the adaptation column: Xenium Prime 5K (5,001 genes) on 21 FFPE trephines (4 control, 2 MGUS, 5 smouldering, 10 myeloma; GEO GSE299193), 15 to 255 genes detected per cell, no perturbations, no protein imaging, four prior gene sets (Wnt / adhesion, plasma-cell stress, T-cell exhaustion, cytotoxicity).

Two corrections to the brief, found during verification:

- "Learning Causal Representations of Single Cells via Sparse Mechanism Shift Modeling" (Lopez, Tagasovska, Ra, Cho, Pritchard, Regev) is in PMLR volume 213, Proceedings of the Second Conference on Causal Learning and Reasoning (CLeaR 2023), pages 662 to 691, not NeurIPS 2023 (verified at https://proceedings.mlr.press/v213/lopez23a.html; the neurips.cc listing is a 2022 workshop entry). It is therefore listed under fallbacks, not under the top-venue table.
- The NeurIPS 2023 paper on the same theme is the SAMS-VAE paper by Bereket and Karaletsos (verified, see A.1).

### A.3 Recommendation for A

No ICML / ICLR / NeurIPS main-conference paper from 2023 to 2026 was found that does what Spectra or expiMap do, namely factorise observational expression into programs seeded by user-supplied gene sets. The closest top-venue design is SENA-discrepancy-VAE (ICLR 2025), whose gene-set-masked encoder can be lifted into an observational VAE, but its causal guarantees do not apply without perturbations. Recommended plan, stated plainly:

1. Primary: Spectra (Nature Biotechnology; biology venue) with the four prior sets plus a Hallmark subset restricted to the 5,001-gene panel, run per cell type with patient as a covariate. This is the fallback because no top-venue method fits.
2. Top-venue component: a SENA-style gene-set-masked encoder (ICLR 2025 architecture) trained observationally, reported as "SENA-style encoder, causal identifiability not claimed".
3. Unsupervised control: cNMF (eLife 2019) to confirm that the seeded programs also appear without priors.

### B.3 Recommendation for B

HEIST (ICLR 2026) is the only top-venue method found whose representation is explicitly gene-level and spatially contextual, which is what a spatial gene program needs; it should be used as a fine-tuned spatial gene-embedding backbone if weights are released. For the factorisation itself, no top-venue method anchors spatial factors to prior gene sets, so the stated fallback is NicheCompass (Nature Genetics 2025), run with the four prior sets added as custom gene programs, cross-checked by NSF (Nature Methods 2023) on per-cell-type count matrices. Hotspot provides the simplest spatial module test and should be the first thing run.

### E.3 Recommendation for E

Use a negative-binomial multi-output head in the scVI parameterisation (Nature Methods 2018; mean = size factor times softmax decoder, gene-wise or cell-wise dispersion) as the primary count likelihood for morphology-to-gene regression, with zero inflation tested and reported rather than assumed (DCA, Nature Communications 2019). Top-venue alternative: DDPN (ICML 2025) as a second head, since it is the only 2023 to 2026 main-conference count-regression method with input-dependent dispersion found. For program-score targets (continuous), use the natural-parameter heteroscedastic Gaussian of Immer et al. (NeurIPS 2023).
